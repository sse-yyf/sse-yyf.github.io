The Cost of Trying: Evidence from Field and Experiment

Job Market Paper

Manuscript (PDF)
Abstract

Switching costs often delay the adoption of new market alternatives. Although such delays are commonly attributed to forward-looking utility maximization, they may equally stem from behavioral inertia and a disproportionate weighting of immediate costs. We disentangle these explanations in repeated strategic environments using field evidence and a preregistered experiment. In field data from Hearthstone, resource barriers temporarily depress the exploration of newly introduced strategies and slow competitive adjustment when counter-strategies are costly to acquire. To evaluate people’s behavior against an explicit benchmark, we design a repeated asymmetric zero-sum game with an expanding action set and varying switching costs. The aggregate adoption exhibits gradual catch-up under low costs but remains locked at low rates under high costs. By role, while deterring Row adoption theoretically amplifies Column entry incentives, high costs suppress Column adoption as well, leaving the strategic gains generated by opponent inertia largely unexploited. To capture these dynamics, we extend Experience-Weighted Attraction (EWA) model to incorporate switching costs (EWA-C). Compared with forward-looking mixed logit models and machine learning models, EWA-C achieves the most accurate out-of-sample predictions, emphasizing the importance of bounded rationality in market transitions.
